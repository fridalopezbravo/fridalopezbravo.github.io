Welcome! I am a senior at Harvard University studying Government and Mathematics. I work in comparative politics with a regional focus on Latin America.

My current research is about the politics of counting. I study why states that have the capacity to measure something, and are required to, sometimes choose not to measure it well, especially when accurate numbers would expose their own failures. My senior thesis examines this question through Mexico’s national registry of the disappeared. I am also interested in state building and weak institutions, forbearance, and crimes committed by the state. I use quantitative and mixed methods, from web scraping and statistical modeling to archival research and interviews.

I grew up in Mexico City and speak Spanish, English, French and Portuguese. I have 5 dogs and 3 cats back in Mexico among which my favorites are my two chihuahuas, Churro and Tequila.

To Disappear Twice: Politics of Counting Around Forced Disappearances in Mexico

Senior thesis, 2026–2027 · Advisor: Yanilda González

I explain why Mexico's national registry of the disappeared remains unreliable despite a legal mandate and sustained domestic and international pressure. I argue that registry opacity is politically produced: where accurate data would expose state failure, officials sustain managed legibility through politically mediated capacity constraints, strategic non-enforcement, and administrative blockage.

Data: an original panel of weekly registry scrapes, freedom-of-information requests, and elite and civil society interviews.
